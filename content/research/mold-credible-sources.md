## 2. Key public takeaways for homeowners (by education pillar)

### What mold is
- Mold is a natural part of the outdoor environment; spores are tiny and travel on air, clothing, pets, and HVAC pathways (**CDC**, **EPA**).
- Indoors, mold grows when spores land on a **wet or damp** surface that also provides a food source (paper, wood, dust, drywall, fabric, etc.) (**EPA**, **CDC**, **UF/IFAS**).
- **None of the common molds grow without moisture** — moisture control is the practical lever (**EPA**, **Florida DOH**).
- It is not realistic (or necessary) to eliminate all indoor spores; the goal is to stop **growth** by keeping materials dry (**EPA**).
- Common indoor genera mentioned by CDC include *Cladosporium*, *Penicillium*, and *Aspergillus* — knowing the genus is **not** required before cleanup (**CDC**).

### How to spot
- Look for discoloration/spots of many colors and a **musty odor** (**CDC**).
- Suspect hidden mold when there is a persistent musty smell, known water damage, or symptoms reported with no visible source — possible locations include behind drywall/wallpaper, under carpet/pad, around leaking pipes, inside ducts, roof leaks above ceilings (**EPA**).
- Condensation on windows, walls, or pipes is an early moisture signal — dry quickly and reduce the source (**EPA**, **Florida DOH**).
- After flooding or long wet periods (&gt;24–48 hours), assume mold risk even if not yet obvious (**CDC** disaster guide, **FL DOH** factsheet).
- Routine air sampling is **not** recommended by CDC; EPA notes sampling is usually unnecessary when visible mold is present, and there are **no federal numeric mold limits** to “pass/fail” a home (**CDC**, **EPA**). Florida DOH likewise does not provide mold testing.

### DIY vs pro
- **EPA rule of thumb:** if the moldy area is less than about **10 square feet** (~3 ft × 3 ft), most healthy adults can often handle cleanup themselves following EPA tips (**EPA**).
- Prefer **professional help** when: area is larger / heavy water damage; sewage or contaminated water; suspected HVAC contamination; hidden/complex growth; or anyone in the home has significant health concerns — consult a health professional before DIY (**EPA**, **CDC**).
- Vulnerable groups (asthma/COPD, mold allergy, immune suppression) should **not** do mold cleanup (**CDC**, **FL DOH** factsheet).
- Small bathroom surface mold that returns with dampness is often managed with regular cleaning and better moisture control (**EPA**).
- Scrub mold from **hard surfaces** with detergent and water; dry completely. Porous materials (ceiling tile, carpet, soaked drywall/insulation) often cannot be fully cleaned and may need discard (**EPA**, **CDC**).
- Do **not** paint or caulk over mold (**EPA**, **CDC**, **FL DOH**).
- In Florida, mold assessors and remediators are subject to state licensure provisions (see Fla. Stat. §468.84–468.8424 via **Florida DOH** mold page) — education content can point readers to official licensure info, not legal advice.

### Prevention
- **Fix leaks** (roof, plumbing, windows, AC drain pans) promptly (**EPA**, **CDC**, **Florida DOH**, **UF/IFAS**).
- Dry wet materials within **24–48 hours** when possible (**EPA**, **CDC**); Florida DOH also stresses cleaning/drying spills within about **24 hours**.
- Keep indoor relative humidity **below 60%**, ideally **30–50%** (**EPA**, **UF/IFAS**); CDC often states **no higher than 50%**. Florida DOH emphasizes **below 60%** with AC or dehumidifier.
- Use bath/kitchen exhaust that vents **outdoors**; vent dryers outdoors (**EPA**, **CDC**, **Florida DOH**).
- Keep AC drip pans clean and drains flowing; maintain HVAC filters (**EPA**, **UF/IFAS**).
- Grade soil away from foundation; manage outdoor sprinkler spray hitting walls (**EPA**, **Florida DOH**).
- After storms/power outages, check indoor humidity once power returns — Florida outdoor humidity is often high, especially at night (**UF/IFAS**).

### Process / remediation principles (S-520 *concepts* only — high level)
> **Copyright note:** ANSI/IICRC S520 is a **paid** consensus standard. Honest Restoration may describe **publicly shared industry/education principles** and link to IICRC’s official S520 page and webstore. Do **not** quote, paraphrase at length, or reconstruct S-520 clauses, tables, or protocols from the paid document.

Publicly useful, non-proprietary framing (aligned with EPA homeowner guidance + IICRC’s *published topic list* on iicrc.org/s520):

1. **Assess moisture** — Find and stop the water/humidity source before or as part of cleanup (**EPA**: cleanup without fixing water → mold returns).
2. **Contain / limit spread** — For larger or disturbed growth, limit spore movement (professional methods; EPA points larger jobs to detailed remediation guidance / experienced contractors).
3. **Remove** — Clean hard surfaces; remove contaminated porous materials that cannot be dried and cleaned (**EPA**, **CDC**).
4. **Dry** — Dry the structure and contents thoroughly; use closed-space dehumidification when electricity is safe (**FL DOH** / **CDC** disaster tips).
5. **Verify** — Moisture problem fixed; no visible mold or musty odor; area can be re-occupied without new complaints — EPA frames this as a practical judgment, not a lab “clearance number” (**EPA**). IICRC publicly lists **post-remediation verification** and related professional topics among S520 components — link out; do not invent checklist text from the standard.

IICRC’s public S520 page also lists (names only): principles of mold remediation; cleaners/antimicrobials/coatings as tools; building/material science; remediator qualifications; safety & health; documentation; inspection & preliminary determination; structural / HVAC / contents remediation; indoor environmental professional — useful for “what professionals study,” not for copying procedures.

### Florida humidity
- Florida’s warm, humid climate means outdoor air often carries more moisture than homes need; **opening windows** during humid weather can raise indoor RH (**UF/IFAS**).
- Average outdoor RH in Florida is often cited around the mid-70%s; nights can reach **80–100%** RH (**UF/IFAS** Extension education).
- Indoor targets used in public guidance cluster around **≤50–55%** ideal comfort/prevention, and **&lt;60%** as a widely cited upper bound (**EPA**, **CDC**, **Florida DOH**, **UF/IFAS**).
- Air conditioning removes moisture while cooling, but in hot-humid climates cooling load and dehumidification needs can diverge — supplemental dehumidification and leak-tight envelopes matter (**UF/IFAS**; NIST building-science literature on cooling-climate walls).
- Storm surge, tropical rainfall, and AC condensate are recurring Florida moisture sources (**Florida DOH**).
- After hurricanes/power loss: dry quickly, discard what can’t be cleaned/dried in 24–48 hrs, and have flooded HVAC checked before restart (**FL DOH** factsheet, **CDC**).

## 5. Disclaimer notes for the site

Suggested site-wide / page-foot language (adapt to legal review; keep calm tone):

1. **Not medical advice.** Content on Honest Restoration is for general mold and moisture education only. It is not a diagnosis, treatment plan, or personal health recommendation. Anyone with health concerns related to dampness or mold should consult a licensed physician or other qualified clinician.
2. **Remediator ≠ doctor.** A mold assessor, remediator, or restoration contractor addresses **buildings and materials**, not medical conditions. Credentialed cleanup professionals are not a substitute for medical care.
3. **Follow current official guidance.** Practices and public recommendations change. Readers should rely on the latest materials from the **EPA**, **CDC**, **Florida Department of Health**, and other primary authorities linked on this site—not on outdated screenshots or secondhand summaries.
4. **Licensing & local rules.** Florida regulates certain mold-related professions; property and insurance situations vary. This site does not provide legal, insurance, or licensing advice.
5. **No scare framing.** Presence of mold or humidity does not automatically mean a home is unsafe; the consistent public message is: **control moisture, clean what you can safely clean, and get qualified help when the job exceeds DIY scope.**
6. **S-520 attribution.** Mentions of professional remediation standards refer readers to **IICRC’s official S520 information and purchase pages**. Honest Restoration does not reproduce the copyrighted standard.
